# Security review — T-0061: Decisions raised by T-0060 (ADR-0007)
Date: 2026-11-18 · Reviewer agent: security-reviewer · Scope: `git diff yasir` on branch `task/T-0061-t0060-decisions` (uncommitted): `docs/adr/0007-decisions-from-the-ia-consistency-pass.md` (new) and the PRD, register, generator and task changes it makes. Questions 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17 and 18 were checked in depth; the others were read for security impact. They were checked against `identity-access`, `admin-governance`, `admin-panel`, `security`, `privacy-audit`, `fraud-risk`, `seller-dashboard`, `notifications-messaging`, the sitemap and the admin page specs, and ADR-0005 point 3.2. · Follows up: —

## Verdict
changes required

None of the 17 decisions loosens a control or grants access beyond the stricter reading of the current documents. Each one takes the recommended or a stricter option. Questions 1, 2, 3, 7, 12, 14, 16, 17 and 18 narrow or tighten. Question 9 is correctly left undecided and held as `blocked` task T-0064. Two major findings remain. First, the new step-up rule for Customers can be bypassed by adding a first factor (F-SEC-017). Second, the ADR states an interim restriction on the notification delivery log that two binding documents contradict (F-SEC-016).

## Findings
| ID | Severity | Location | Finding | Recommended fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F-SEC-016 | major | ADR-0007 Consequences ("Until then `AD-NOT-LOG` and `AD-SET-NOTIF` are reachable by Super Admin only"); `sitemap.md` row `AD-NOT-LOG` (Role: Support); `notifications-messaging.md` NTF-5 ("visible to Support in the delivery log") | Question 9 is correctly reserved, but the interim restriction the ADR states is not what the documents say. The sitemap and NTF-5 still grant Support access to the delivery log, which holds recipient personal data, masked. So three documents give two answers on an access rule about personal data. **Failure scenario:** an implementer builds the permission list from the sitemap role column or from NTF-5 and grants Support the delivery log and "Resend a transactional message". In effect that decides question 9 in the looser direction before the owner has ruled, while ADR-0007 tells the owner that only Super Admin has access. The ADR also says question 9 "grants access", even though NTF-5 already grants it, so the owner receives an inaccurate picture of the current state. | Make the documents agree. Either mark `AD-NOT-LOG` (sitemap and page spec) and NTF-5 as "Super Admin only until T-0064", which is the stricter reading and a tightening Claude may make, or correct the ADR and T-0064 to say that Support read access already exists under NTF-5 and the owner is asked to confirm or remove it. Also name `AD-NOT-CAMP` (Marketing) in the T-0064 scope. |
| F-SEC-017 | major | `identity-access.md` SES-5 (new factor sentence); Step-up prompt in `shared-components.md` | SES-5 makes the step-up factor "the password or 2FA where the account has one", and an OTP to the verified phone only when the account has neither. Nothing requires step-up to *add* a first password, add 2FA, or add a first email (SES-5 lists "email or phone change", and adding is not clearly a change). **Failure scenario:** someone holds a stolen Customer session (a 30-day sliding web session or a 60-day mobile refresh token) for a phone-only account. They set a password, which the account now "has". They step up with that password and change the phone number to their own; under AUTH-5 the notice goes only to the old number. Or they add a refund account and redirect COD refunds (R-13). The OTP step-up that question 15 introduces is bypassed, and the takeover is permanent. | In SES-5, require step-up with the account's *existing* factor (an OTP to the verified phone for phone-only accounts) before setting a first password, enrolling or replacing 2FA, and adding or changing an email. State that a factor added in the current session cannot satisfy a step-up in that session. |
| F-SEC-018 | minor | `identity-access.md` SES-5; `seller-dashboard.md` SDB-3; `privacy-audit.md` DSR-2; `payments.md` PAY-17 | (a) "Password or 2FA where the account has one" can be read as letting a password alone satisfy step-up for sellers and admins, who always have both. That conflicts with SDB-3 ("re-authentication with 2FA") and weakens the step-up before admin dual-control approvals. (b) SES-5 is the authoritative list, but it omits two step-ups other rules need: data-subject access or export requests (DSR-2) and adding or changing a Customer refund account (PAY-17, R-13). "Payout destination" reads as a seller term. **Failure scenario:** someone who has a seller's or admin's password and a live session approves a dual-control request or invites staff without the second factor. Or an implementation following SES-5 exports a Customer's personal data without step-up. | Reword SES-5. Where 2FA is enrolled (always for sellers and admins), the step-up factor is 2FA. A Customer without 2FA uses the password, or else an OTP to the verified phone. Add DSR access or export requests and refund-account addition or change to the list, or state that the list includes the step-ups named in DSR-2, SDB-3 and PAY-17. |
| F-SEC-019 | minor | `identity-access.md` SES-5 (Customer OTP step-up), AUTH-5 (phone change), §4.2 | For phone-only Customers, the OTP step-up and the phone-change OTP both go to the same SIM. This is better than the previous state, which had no step-up, but it gives no protection against a SIM swap. Phone change then turns a temporary SIM swap into a permanent takeover, because the "old number notified" message goes to the attacker-held SIM and recovery is by OTP to that same phone. A Customer who has lost their number cannot pass the step-up at all, and no recovery path is defined (ADM-6 rightly bars Support from changing identifiers). **Failure scenario:** a fraudster swaps the victim's SIM, signs in by OTP, steps up by OTP and moves the account to a number they own. They add a refund account, and the victim has no way back in. | For phone change on accounts without a password or 2FA: also notify and allow revert through a verified email where one exists (as for email change). Hold refund-account changes and the release of points or balances for a cool-down after a phone change. Require the ID-REC-1 second factor (details of a past order) where available. Bind step-up OTPs to a distinct purpose and name the action in the SMS text. Define the lost-number path (for example, a new account per ID-REC-1). |
| F-SEC-020 | minor | `admin-governance.md` §5 (new sentence); `pages-admin-finance-risk.md` `AD-RISK-COD`; `pages-admin-growth-settings.md` `AD-LOY-CONFIG`, `AD-SET-LOYALTY`; dual-control table §3 | (a) §5 now says every `cfg:` value is edited by the Super Admin except the commission schedule. But `AD-RISK-COD` lets Fraud/Risk edit the `cfg:cod.*` risk limits, and `AD-LOY-CONFIG` lets Marketing edit loyalty rates. The question 8 rationale ("Risk already sets COD eligibility upstream") relies on the first of these. So the documents disagree on who controls fraud limits. (b) "The owner role ... reviews changes" has no defined mechanism. (c) §5 puts the "financially sensitive settings" under dual control, but §3 names an approver only for thresholds, commission and tax. Payout cycle, escrow hold, COD limits, the COD fee and shipping rates have none. `AD-SET-LOYALTY` offers a plain "Edit" of the same values that `AD-LOY-CONFIG` changes only under dual control. **Failure scenario:** two Super Admins, or one Super Admin through `AD-SET-LOYALTY`, change escrow hold, COD caps or loyalty earn rates without Finance or Risk ever seeing the change before it takes effect. | In §5, list the domain pages that edit their own `cfg:` group (`AD-RISK-COD`, `AD-LOY-CONFIG`, `AD-SET-COMM`). Define "reviews" (for example, a notification to the owner role plus an approver role in §3). Add §3 rows naming the approver for each financially sensitive setting (Finance for money values, Risk for COD risk limits). State that every page editing a value applies the same dual-control rule, so `AD-SET-LOYALTY` cannot bypass `AD-LOY-CONFIG`. |
| F-SEC-021 | minor | `pages-seller-operations.md` `SL-AUTH-VERIFY`; FL-8 | Question 13 makes the phone-OTP state reachable after sign-in while the phone is unverified, before 2FA is enrolled. In that state the session rests on a password alone. The specs do not say whether the unverified phone number can be edited in this state, or whether sign-in is possible before the email link is confirmed. **Failure scenario:** someone who has a new seller's password, from reuse or phishing, signs in during onboarding, replaces the unverified phone with their own, verifies it and enrols 2FA. They now own the seller account before KYC, and the real owner is locked out of 2FA recovery. | State that sign-in reaches `SL-AUTH-VERIFY` only after the email is verified. State that changing the phone in this state requires a fresh email link and notifies the email. AUTH-7 and §4.1 send limits apply as for registration. |
| F-SEC-022 | minor | `seller-accounts-kyc.md` SEL-6; `ia-model.md` IA-10 | Store slugs are now derived from the name and change with it, and old slugs redirect. Nothing says that a previous slug stays reserved, or that platform terms are reserved slugs. **Failure scenario:** store A renames itself, and its old slug, which still circulates in shared links and ads, becomes free. Store B registers a name that derives to that slug and captures A's traffic and reputation. Or a seller registers a name that derives to `nyazek`, `official` or `support`. | State that a store's previous slugs remain reserved to that store for as long as they redirect, and are never reassigned. Keep a reserved-slug list (platform name, "official", "support", admin and route words) that the derivation refuses. |
| F-SEC-023 | note | `identity-access.md` §4 and the new row in `admin-governance.md` §3 | The new dual-control row for an admin password or 2FA reset matches §4. It does not exclude the target account from being the requester or the approver, and with the DC-3 minimum of two Super Admins, a reset of one leaves no different second approver except through break-glass. | Consider adding: "the target of a reset can be neither requester nor approver". Also state that a reset of a Super Admin, when only two exist, uses the break-glass procedure (§4). |
| F-SEC-024 | note | `web-apps.md` §4.3; `SF-PDP` (question 11) | The `410` page with `noindex` is sound. CAT-12 archiving also covers products removed for prohibited content or IP and trademark complaints. The "No longer available" page should not keep showing the original title, images or description in those cases. | When the product spec is written, state that a product archived for a policy or legal reason shows only the generic notice and similar products. |
| F-SEC-025 | note | ADR-0007 questions 3 and 5 | Question 3: engineers get metrics and alerts (outbox age, dead-letter growth) from the observability backend, not dead-letter contents. Payloads must stay out of traces and logs (SEC-21). Question 5: STK-6 exposes exact stock counts at or below 5 to competitors. That is an accepted business signal and creates no personal-data or money risk. | No action. Keep SEC-21 in mind when the dead-letter tooling is designed. |
