## Verdict
changes required

All thirteen earlier findings are addressed, and the major F-ARCH-017 is fixed properly: the Worker signs the edge receipt time, and the clarification to API-11 and PAY-6 is listed for the owner. Two earlier minor findings are only partly resolved, and one of them now carries a major problem. To fix F-ARCH-024, the revision says that moving off Cloudflare would be a planned migration, not a switch. That leaves reliability-sre §4.5 ("edge provider down: documented DNS switch to the alternative path") unmet, and the ADR does not put that to the owner as a deviation. The Risks row still claims a "DNS fallback" (F-ARCH-030). The other new findings are minor: the point-in-time copy's key is not recorded as a BKP-1 deviation, the replay envelope loses the provider source IP that API-11 checks, and it is not said where `queue_owner` maintenance runs. There is also one note on task-document consistency.

## Findings
| ID | Severity | Location | Finding | Recommended fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F-ARCH-030 | major | ADR-0004, Consequences bullet 5 ("moving to CloudFront is a planned migration, not a switch … webhook buffering falls back to provider retries and reconciliation until rebuilt"); Risks row "Cloudflare as a second infrastructure vendor" ("CDN port; DNS fallback"); reliability-sre §4.5 | reliability-sre §4.5 requires a provider outage playbook for "edge provider down (documented DNS switch to the alternative path)". The revision now says correctly that no ready alternative path exists. Cloudflare serves DNS, CDN, WAF, Turnstile, Waiting Room, zero-trust Admin access, the static Seller and Admin apps, the pinned `api` certificate and the webhook buffer. The ALB accepts only Cloudflare ranges and Cloudflare's origin-pull certificate. Because Cloudflare is also the authoritative DNS, a "DNS switch" during a Cloudflare outage would itself need a registrar name-server change or a secondary DNS provider. So the ADR does not meet §4.5. It does not say so, the requirement mapping does not cover §4.5, and the Risks row still claims a DNS fallback that the Consequences bullet contradicts. A known gap against a PRD requirement cannot be accepted implicitly. The project rules require it to go to the owner. | Choose one and record it. (1) Define the minimum alternative path that §4.5 needs and its pre-provisioned parts, such as a secondary DNS provider or registrar-level plan, an alternative ALB listener and trust configuration, S3 copies of the SPA builds, and what stays unavailable (Turnstile, Waiting Room, webhook buffer). (2) State that §4.5's DNS switch is not provided at launch, with the accepted outage impact, and list it in Consequences as a PRD clarification for the owner to accept. In both cases, correct the Risks row and map §4.5 in the requirement table. |
| F-ARCH-031 | minor | ADR-0004, "Backups" row "Point-in-time recovery, 35 days" ("the RDS key in the primary account (see the copy below for key separation)"); mapping row "BKP-1, BKP-2"; Consequences (infrastructure-devops §7 update) | BKP-1 says that backups, without exception, are "encrypted with keys separate from the production data keys". The point-in-time copy is now immutable but still uses the production RDS key. The ADR argues that key separation is satisfied at the asset level by the snapshot copy. That may be a reasonable reading, but it is an interpretation of BKP-1, not compliance with it. Unlike the API-11 clarification, it is not listed in Consequences for the owner, and the BKP-1 mapping row still cites the whole section as compliant. | Add one sentence to the Consequences item that updates infrastructure-devops §7: BKP-1 key separation is met by the cross-account copies, and the 35-day point-in-time copy uses the production key. The owner then accepts that reading explicitly. |
| F-ARCH-032 | minor | ADR-0004, "Edge buffering of webhooks" points 2–4; API-11 ("applies an IP allow-list where the provider publishes one") | (a) The signed envelope carries only the edge receipt time. A replayed request reaches the origin from the replay consumer, not from the provider, so the adapter cannot apply API-11's provider IP allow-list on replay, and the original client IP is lost. Either the Worker enforces the allow-list at receipt, or the signed envelope must also carry the original source IP. (b) Point 3 says the consumer replays "once it is healthy", but no mechanism gates replay on origin health. If the consumer simply retries against a down origin, a fixed retry limit can be used up long before the 4-hour regional recovery ends. Every buffered webhook would then go to the dead-letter queue, and the buffer would add nothing over provider retries. | (a) State where the IP allow-list is checked for buffered requests (at the Worker, or on replay with the source IP inside the signed envelope), and include it in the same API-11 clarification. (b) State that retry delays and the retry limit together cover at least the 4-hour recovery time, or that replay is paused until an origin health check passes. Detailed design stays in T-0029. |
| F-ARCH-033 | minor | ADR-0004, "Events and jobs across role boundaries" step 4 ("`queue_owner` also creates queues (at deploy, in the migration step) and runs pg-boss maintenance (archiving and purging)"); EVT-7 | Queue creation runs in the migration step, but pg-boss archiving and purging are recurring runtime tasks. EVT-7's monthly partitions of `event_log`, with 25-month retention, also need partitions created and dropped regularly. The ADR does not say which process holds `queue_owner` credentials at runtime. If the `worker` holds them, the worker can update or delete `event_log` rows, and the append-only guarantee of EVT-7 and the least-privilege split in steps 3–4 become nominal. | State that maintenance and partition management run as a separate scheduled task (or job) with `queue_owner` credentials that the `api` and `worker` roles cannot read. Alternatively, name a narrow maintenance role that has no update or delete on `event_log` rows beyond dropping expired partitions. |
