## Verdict
changes required

Summary: The rewrite fixes both critical findings. Protected-path plans, the human reviews, migrations and closure stay with the owner (point 3.1). Loosening any control is reserved, with "when in doubt, reserved" (point 3.2). Production, secrets, reach-widening tools and money or third-party open decisions are reserved, and a table states which earlier rules are kept or superseded. One major gap remains from F-SEC-004: Claude can still lower the severity of a reviewer's finding, or dismiss a critical or major architecture finding, and so avoid the block. There are also two minor wording defects and two notes.

## Findings

| ID | Severity | Location | Finding | Recommended fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F-SEC-012 | major | ADR-0005 point 2.4 (last sentence); `docs/reviews/README.md` ("A finding judged invalid…", severity scale); ADR-0003 decisions 1 and 3 | **The author can still decide whether a finding blocks, by lowering its severity or by dismissing an architecture finding.** Point 2.4 stops Claude from dismissing a critical or major *security* finding as invalid. It does not stop Claude from recording it as `minor`, and under the reviews README a `minor` finding may be "consciously deferred" without blocking `done`. It also does not cover critical or major findings from the `architecture-reviewer`, which point 2.4 makes mandatory for ledger, payouts, schema and migrations. The original F-SEC-004 named severity explicitly. Point 3.1 protects protected-path tasks, because the owner reads the reports. It does not protect delegated security work outside protected paths: personal data, file uploads, dependencies, secrets handling, pricing and fees. **Scenario:** for a delegated task that adds an upload endpoint for seller documents, the security reviewer reports a major finding: the content type is not checked and files are served from the application origin. Claude records the finding as minor ("staging only, defer"), opens a backlog task and sets the original task to `done`. No human sees it, and the upload hole ships with the next phase. | In point 2.4, replace the last sentence with: "Claude does not mark a critical or major finding from the `security-reviewer` or the `architecture-reviewer` invalid, lower its severity, or defer it. A dispute about validity or severity keeps the task `blocked` until the owner rules (ADR-0003 decision 3)." Add the same sentence, or a pointer to it, under "From finding to task" in `docs/reviews/README.md`. |
| F-SEC-013 | minor | ADR-0005 point 3.2; T-0061 (questions 3, 14 and 15 of the T-0060 Disposition) | **"Loosens a control" has no stated baseline, and the list of controls leaves out privacy.** (a) Point 3.2 reserves loosening but does not say loosening compared with what, when the PRD is silent or contradicts itself. T-0061, which Claude decides itself, contains exactly such questions. Question 14 asks whether to extend SES-5 step-up to staff invite and removal, account closure, disabling 2FA and account deletion, "or remove those step-ups from the specs". Question 3 asks whether engineers reach system health through a role or through break-glass. Question 15 asks which step-up factor to use for phone-OTP Customers. If the weaker document counts as the baseline, removing the step-ups or granting a standing engineer role reads as "resolving a contradiction", not loosening, and is delegated. (b) The list after the colon reads as exhaustive. It names security.md and dual-control and access rules, but not `privacy-audit.md` (retention, masking, personal-data access audit) or claude-workflow §4 beyond the protected-path list. "When in doubt" helps but depends on the agent noticing the doubt. | Add to point 3.2: "Where the PRD, a page specification or an ADR disagree about a control, or are silent about one, the stricter reading is the baseline, and choosing a weaker option is reserved. The list above is not exhaustive. It includes [privacy-audit](../prd/05-trust/privacy-audit.md) and claude-workflow §4." In the T-0061 task file, mark questions 3, 14 and 15 as reserved unless the stricter option is chosen. |
| F-SEC-014 | minor | `CLAUDE.md` rule "Nothing unrecorded" | **`CLAUDE.md`, which every session loads, states a narrower rule than ADR-0005.** "No new dependency, tool, service or datastore without a recorded decision under ADR-0005 (and the owner's approval when it costs money)" implies that only cost triggers the owner. Point 3.11 also reserves free MCP servers, plugins, skills, GitHub Actions, CI services, dependencies on protected paths, and packages with install scripts. Point 3.5 reserves creating accounts. The second sentence, "Do not weaken or delete tests, lint rules or checks to make a change pass", keeps the "to make a change pass" qualifier that point 4 removed ("for a change or on their own"). A session that follows `CLAUDE.md` without opening the ADR gets the looser rule, for example enabling a free MCP server after recording a decision. | Change the rule to: "No new dependency, tool, service or datastore without a recorded decision and a security review under ADR-0005; the categories in its points 3.5 and 3.11 need the owner. Tests, lint rules, CI gates and checks are never weakened, skipped or deleted (ADR-0005 point 4)." |
| F-SEC-015 | note | `tasks/MASTER_TASKS.md` T-0063 and Phase 5; ADR-0005 Consequences | **Technical enforcement is not tied to the start of implementation, and T-0063's status is inconsistent.** T-0063 is `ready` while its dependency T-0062 is `ready`, not `done`, which contradicts ADR-0003 decision 4. The Consequences say T-0063 comes "before implementation opens", but neither T-0053 nor Phase 5 depends on it. Kickoff (D-15) is reserved, so the owner is present when implementation starts.
