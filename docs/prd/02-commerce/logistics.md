## 1. Model

Nyazek contracts the couriers, sets customer shipping fees, generates waybills and pays courier invoices. Sellers never integrate with or choose a courier.

- LOG-1 The courier for each shipment is assigned automatically from the serviceability matrix (primary, then fallback). v6 said both that sellers "do not choose" and that they "select from an approved list"; automatic assignment is the rule.
- LOG-2 All courier integrations sit behind one `CourierPort`. Adding or replacing a courier needs no seller-side change.

### 1.1 Courier integration tiers

Iraqi couriers differ widely in technical capability. Each courier adapter declares its tier:

| Tier | Capability | Nyazek handling |
|---|---|---|
| A | API for waybill creation, tracking webhooks or polling, proof of delivery, COD statement | Fully automated |
| B | API for waybills and tracking polling only | Automated shipping; COD statement by file upload |
| C | No API | Waybill numbers from a pre-allocated range, labels generated by Nyazek, status and COD updates by CSV upload or a simple courier portal page operated by Logistics |

Tier C is a P0 capability so that launch does not depend on courier APIs. Launch couriers and their tiers are decision D-28.

## 2. Entities

| Entity | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `Courier` | Contract, tier, credentials, SLA targets, COD remittance terms, exposure cap |
| `ServiceArea` | Courier × governorate × district: service level, SLA days, COD allowed, weight and size limits, priority |
| `Shipment` | One per sub-order: courier, waybill number, parcel weight and dimensions, pickup address, status, attempts, POD |
| `TrackingEvent` | Raw courier event and its normalised status |
| `Pickup` | Requested slot or declared drop-off for one or more shipments |
| `ReturnShipment` | Reverse shipment for an approved return |
| `CodRemittance` | A courier's payment of collected cash, with per-waybill lines |

## 3. Serviceability

- SVC-1 Every governorate (19) and district has a configuration: serviceable or not, courier(s) in priority order, SLA, COD availability, weight and size limits, perishables support. At launch the four Kurdistan Region governorates (Erbil, Duhok, Sulaymaniyah, Halabja) are configured as not serviceable until D-13 is closed (SVC-4); addresses there are rejected under SVC-2 with a "not yet delivering to this area" message.
- SVC-2 An address outside any service area is rejected at checkout ([checkout-orders](checkout-orders.md) ADR-3).
- SVC-3 Delivery targets: Baghdad 1–2 days; other governorates 3–7 days. The estimate shown to the customer = seller handling time + service-area SLA from the offer's origin to the destination.
- SVC-4 Kurdistan Region coverage is announced only after logistics, licensing and customs rules are validated (D-13).
- SVC-5 Items flagged `hazardous` ([catalog-listings](catalog-listings.md) §2.1) are assigned only to couriers and service areas configured to accept that hazard type; where none exists the item is shown as not deliverable to that address.
- SVC-6 Couriers, service areas, rate cards and the geo reference dataset belong to a market. Shipments never cross a market border (MKT-3).

## 4. Shipping fees

- FEE-1 Customers pay a Nyazek-set standard fee per sub-order from a rate card keyed by destination zone and parcel weight tier. The rate card and free-delivery threshold are decision D-21.
- FEE-2 Free delivery applies when the order's product subtotal across all sellers, after seller and platform coupons and before loyalty redemption, reaches the threshold ([checkout-orders](checkout-orders.md) §4.1).
- FEE-3 Heavy or oversized items carry a surcharge that applies even when delivery is otherwise free.
- FEE-4 Nyazek absorbs the difference between courier cost and fees collected. This subsidy is a line in the unit-economics model ([business-model](../01-product/business-model.md) §4).
- FEE-5 The declared package weight and dimensions on the variant drive the fee. A courier re-weigh that differs materially creates an exception against the seller's listing data.

## 5. Shipment lifecycle

1. Seller confirms the sub-order, packs it and marks it ready: the system assigns the courier, creates the waybill and produces the label, packing slip and receipt; the sub-order becomes `READY_TO_SHIP`, which meets the shipment SLA.
2. Seller requests a pickup slot from one of their pickup addresses or declares drop-off at a courier hub (where supported), within the handover grace ([checkout-orders](checkout-orders.md) §7).
3. Courier scans at handover → sub-order `SHIPPED`.
4. Tracking events update the internal timeline; the customer sees the internal timeline plus the courier's tracking number and link.
5. Delivery attempt(s) → `DELIVERED` with proof of delivery, or `DELIVERY_FAILED`.

- SHP-1 Courier statuses are mapped to internal statuses through a per-courier mapping table. Unknown statuses are stored, parked for review and never change order state.
- SHP-2 Events are processed idempotently and in event-time order; an older event arriving late never moves a shipment backwards.
- SHP-3 Courier webhook silence beyond `cfg:logistics.webhook_silence_minutes` (default 60) during operating hours alerts Logistics and switches the adapter to polling.
- SHP-4 If the primary courier rejects or cannot collect a shipment, Logistics (or an automatic rule) reassigns it to the fallback courier with a new waybill.
- SHP-5 Phone serial numbers (IMEI) are captured at `READY_TO_SHIP` for categories that require them.
- SHP-6 The label shows the recipient name, phone, structured address and landmark. Sellers see the customer's contact details only as needed to pack and hand over; phone numbers are masked in the Seller Dashboard list views.

## 6. Delivery attempts and proof of delivery

- DLV-1 At most `cfg:logistics.max_attempts` (default 3) attempts, each preceded by a call or SMS. The customer may reschedule once.
- DLV-2 Proof of delivery is mandatory. Preferred: a delivery OTP sent to the customer and entered by the courier. Accepted fallbacks by courier capability: recipient signature, photo, courier-app confirmation with geolocation.
- DLV-3 The proof type is stored on the shipment and weighs in non-delivery disputes: OTP proof is conclusive unless fraud is shown; weaker proof shifts the burden toward the courier.
- DLV-4 `DELIVERED` starts the escrow hold and the return window.
- DLV-5 The customer refusing the parcel, or being unreachable for all attempts, results in `DELIVERY_FAILED` and return to origin (RTO).

## 7. Failed delivery: cost attribution

One rule replaces the three conflicting statements in v6.

| Cause | Outbound + return shipping cost borne by | Other consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Seller fault (parcel not ready, wrong item data, damaged packing) | Seller (wallet debit JE-19) | Counts in seller metrics |
| Customer refusal or unreachable | Nyazek | Customer risk score and COD eligibility affected; for prepaid orders see ORD-9 / D-31 |
| Courier fault (lost, damaged, missed SLA) | Courier, per contract | Courier performance; claim process |
| Undetermined | Nyazek | – |

The cause is recorded by Logistics from courier reason codes, with evidence; the seller can contest a seller-fault attribution through the appeal flow.

## 8. COD remittance controls

- COD-1 Each courier has a remittance SLA (`cfg:cod.remittance_sla_days` (default 3) after delivery unless the contract differs) and an exposure cap on collected-but-unremitted cash.
- COD-2 When a courier's outstanding COD exceeds its cap, new COD shipments are routed to the fallback courier and Logistics and Finance are alerted.
- COD-3 Every remittance is matched per waybill ([ledger-reconciliation](../04-finance/ledger-reconciliation.md) §6). Shortfalls are courier liability under the contract.
- COD-4 An ageing report of unremitted COD by courier is available to Logistics and Finance daily.
- COD-5 Exceptions (amount differs, cash lost, delivered-not-remitted) follow a case workflow owned by the Logistics Manager with Finance approval for write-offs.

## 9. Reverse logistics

- An approved return creates a `ReturnShipment`: pickup from the customer's address or drop-off at a partner point.
- 3P returns go back to the seller's return address; 1P returns go to the Nyazek warehouse. An inspection hub for 3P returns is P2.
- Who pays return shipping is defined in [returns-disputes](returns-disputes.md) §3.
- RTO parcels return to the seller's pickup address (the Nyazek warehouse for 1P); the seller confirms receipt (`RTO_RECEIVED`) from the order page within `cfg:logistics.rto_confirm_hours` (default 48) of the courier's delivery scan, otherwise it is auto-confirmed. Confirming records the condition per unit (RET-4) and creates the restock task; auto-confirmation records no condition. Restocking follows STK-5.

## 10. Damage, loss and insurance

- Damaged or lost parcels follow the courier's claim process; the courier's liability limits are part of the contract.
- The customer is refunded without waiting for the claim outcome when the courier confirms loss ([checkout-orders](checkout-orders.md) §6).
- Optional insurance for high-value shipments is P1.

## 11. Courier performance

Tracked per courier and governorate: delivery success rate, on-time rate, average delivery time, RTO rate, pickup failure rate, remittance timeliness, claims. SLA breaches trigger the contract's penalty process and may lead to removal from the approved list.

## 12. Acceptance criteria

- A full order lifecycle can be completed with a Tier C courier using only CSV uploads.
- Replayed and out-of-order tracking events never regress a shipment's status.
- When a courier exceeds its COD exposure cap, the next COD shipment is assigned to the fallback courier.
- Every failed delivery has a recorded cause and the matching cost entry.
